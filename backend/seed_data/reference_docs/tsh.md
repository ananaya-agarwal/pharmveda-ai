# TSH (Thyroid Stimulating Hormone)

TSH is a hormone released by the pituitary gland that signals the thyroid gland to
produce thyroid hormones (T3 and T4), which regulate metabolism throughout the
body. TSH is usually the first test used to screen for thyroid problems.

A typical adult reference range is often roughly 0.4-4.0 mIU/L, though exact
ranges vary by lab. Because TSH and thyroid hormone levels work as a feedback
loop, a higher-than-usual TSH is often associated with an underactive thyroid
(hypothyroidism), since the pituitary produces more TSH to try to stimulate a
sluggish thyroid. A lower-than-usual TSH is often associated with an overactive
thyroid (hyperthyroidism), where thyroid hormone levels are already high and
suppress TSH production.

TSH results are usually interpreted alongside free T4 (and sometimes free T3) to
get a fuller picture, and symptoms like fatigue, weight changes, or changes in
heart rate or temperature tolerance are also relevant context. Any abnormal TSH
result should be discussed with a doctor rather than interpreted alone.
