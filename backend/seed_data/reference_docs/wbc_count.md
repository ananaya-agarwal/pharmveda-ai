# White Blood Cell (WBC) Count

White blood cells are part of the immune system and help the body fight infection
and respond to inflammation. A WBC count measures the total number of these cells
in a blood sample and is a standard part of a complete blood count (CBC).

A typical adult reference range is often around 4,000-11,000 cells per microliter,
though exact ranges vary by lab. A higher-than-usual WBC count is often associated
with infection, inflammation, stress on the body, certain medications like
steroids, or in some cases blood disorders. A lower-than-usual count is often
associated with certain viral infections, some medications, autoimmune
conditions, or bone marrow-related issues.

WBC count alone doesn't indicate the cause of a change - a "differential" that
breaks the count down into specific white cell types (neutrophils, lymphocytes,
etc.) usually gives more insight into what might be happening. Any significantly
abnormal WBC result is best discussed with a doctor, particularly alongside
symptoms such as fever or unusual fatigue.
