# Hemoglobin

Hemoglobin is the protein in red blood cells that carries oxygen from the lungs to
the rest of the body. It is one of the main values reported in a complete blood
count (CBC).

Typical reference ranges are roughly 13.5-17.5 g/dL for adult men and 12.0-15.5
g/dL for adult women, though exact ranges vary by lab. A lower-than-usual
hemoglobin level is often associated with anemia, which can have many underlying
causes including iron deficiency, vitamin deficiencies, chronic disease, or blood
loss. A higher-than-usual level is less common and can be associated with
dehydration, smoking, living at high altitude, or certain lung or bone marrow
conditions.

Hemoglobin is usually interpreted together with other CBC values such as
hematocrit, red blood cell count, and red cell size (MCV) to help narrow down
likely causes. A single abnormal hemoglobin value is not a diagnosis on its own
and should be discussed with a doctor, especially alongside symptoms like fatigue
or shortness of breath.
