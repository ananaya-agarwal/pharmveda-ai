# Sodium

Sodium is the main electrolyte in the fluid outside of cells and plays a central
role in regulating the body's fluid balance, blood pressure, and nerve/muscle
function.

A typical adult reference range is often roughly 135-145 mmol/L. A
lower-than-usual sodium level (hyponatremia) is often associated with drinking
excessive water, certain medications (like some diuretics), heart or kidney
conditions, or hormonal imbalances. A higher-than-usual level (hypernatremia) is
often associated with dehydration, inadequate water intake, or certain
medications.

Sodium is often measured as part of a broader metabolic panel alongside
potassium, chloride, and kidney function tests, since these values are
interrelated and help clarify the underlying cause of an abnormal result.
Significant sodium imbalances can affect brain and nerve function, so notable
abnormal results are generally worth discussing with a doctor promptly.
