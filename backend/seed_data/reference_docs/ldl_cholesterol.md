# LDL Cholesterol

LDL (low-density lipoprotein) is often called "bad cholesterol" because higher
levels are often associated with increased buildup of fatty deposits in artery
walls over time, which can raise cardiovascular risk.

General reference ranges often used are: below 100 mg/dL as optimal, 100-129 mg/dL
as near-optimal, 130-159 mg/dL as borderline high, 160-189 mg/dL as high, and 190
mg/dL or above as very high. However, the "right" target LDL level for any given
person often depends on their broader cardiovascular risk profile - including
blood pressure, smoking status, diabetes, and family history - which is why the
same LDL number can be interpreted differently for different people.

LDL is usually reported alongside HDL cholesterol, triglycerides, and total
cholesterol as part of a lipid panel, and interpreting one number in isolation is
generally less useful than looking at the full panel and personal risk factors
together with a doctor.
