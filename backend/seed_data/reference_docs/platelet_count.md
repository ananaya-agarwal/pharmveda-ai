# Platelet Count

Platelets are small cell fragments in the blood that help it clot to stop
bleeding. A platelet count is a standard part of a complete blood count (CBC).

A typical adult reference range is often around 150,000-450,000 platelets per
microliter, though exact ranges vary by lab. A lower-than-usual count (often
called thrombocytopenia) is often associated with certain viral infections,
some medications, autoimmune conditions, or bone marrow-related issues, and can
increase bruising or bleeding risk if very low. A higher-than-usual count is
often associated with inflammation, iron deficiency, recent surgery, or in some
cases blood disorders, and can occasionally increase clotting risk if very high.

As with other blood counts, platelet levels are best interpreted in the context
of the full CBC, any symptoms (like unusual bruising or bleeding), and trends
over time rather than a single isolated result, and should be discussed with a
doctor.
