# Triglycerides

Triglycerides are a type of fat found in the blood, largely coming from food, and
also produced by the liver. They are used by the body for energy, but persistently
elevated levels are often associated with increased cardiovascular risk, and very
high levels are also associated with risk of pancreatitis.

Commonly used reference ranges are: below 150 mg/dL as normal, 150-199 mg/dL as
borderline high, 200-499 mg/dL as high, and 500 mg/dL or above as very high.
Triglyceride levels can be significantly affected by recent food intake, so this
test is often done after fasting for accurate interpretation.

Elevated triglycerides are often associated with factors such as excess body
weight, high sugar or refined carbohydrate intake, alcohol use, sedentary
lifestyle, poorly controlled diabetes, or certain medications. As with other lipid
markers, results are best interpreted alongside the rest of the lipid panel and
discussed with a doctor.
