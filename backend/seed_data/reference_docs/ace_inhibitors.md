# ACE Inhibitors (e.g., Lisinopril, Enalapril, Ramipril)

ACE (angiotensin-converting enzyme) inhibitors are commonly prescribed for high
blood pressure, heart failure, and to help protect kidney function in some
people with diabetes. They work by relaxing blood vessels, which lowers blood
pressure and reduces the workload on the heart.

A distinctive side effect of this drug class is a dry, persistent cough in some
people, which usually resolves after stopping the medication and is not
dangerous on its own but is often a reason a doctor switches to a related drug
class. ACE inhibitors can also raise potassium levels and affect kidney function
test results, so these are often monitored periodically, especially when
starting or adjusting the dose.

Combining ACE inhibitors with certain other medications - such as some diuretics,
potassium supplements, or NSAIDs - can amplify effects on potassium or kidney
function, which is another reason a doctor should always know the complete list
of medications someone is taking. Dose changes or discontinuation should always
go through a doctor.
