# Vitamin D (25-Hydroxyvitamin D)

Vitamin D helps the body absorb calcium and supports bone health, and also plays a
role in immune function. The most common blood test measures 25-hydroxyvitamin D,
which reflects the body's overall vitamin D stores from sunlight exposure, diet,
and supplements.

Commonly used reference ranges are: below 20 ng/mL as deficient, 20-29 ng/mL as
insufficient, and roughly 30-100 ng/mL as sufficient, though exact cutoffs vary
somewhat between labs and guidelines. Low vitamin D is often associated with
limited sun exposure, certain diets, darker skin (which produces less vitamin D
from sunlight), obesity, or malabsorption conditions, and has been associated
with weaker bones and, in some studies, other health effects still being
researched.

Vitamin D levels can be improved through sunlight exposure, diet, or supplements,
but appropriate dosing depends on the individual starting level and health
history, so any supplementation plan should be discussed with a doctor rather
than self-directed based on a single test result.
