# HbA1c (Glycated Hemoglobin)

HbA1c is a blood test that reflects average blood sugar levels over roughly the past
two to three months, rather than a single point-in-time reading. It works by measuring
the percentage of hemoglobin (the oxygen-carrying protein in red blood cells) that has
sugar attached to it.

A result below 5.7% is generally considered in the typical range for someone without
diabetes. Values between 5.7% and 6.4% are often associated with prediabetes, and
6.5% or higher on more than one test is often used as one criterion associated with
diabetes. Higher HbA1c over time is often associated with a greater risk of long-term
complications affecting the eyes, kidneys, nerves, and blood vessels.

HbA1c can be affected by conditions unrelated to blood sugar, such as certain anemias
or blood disorders, so a single number should always be interpreted alongside other
tests and a person's overall health picture, and discussed with a doctor rather than
read in isolation.
