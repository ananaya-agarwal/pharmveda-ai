# Potassium

Potassium is an electrolyte that plays a key role in how muscles (including the
heart) and nerves function, and in maintaining fluid balance in cells.

A typical adult reference range is often roughly 3.5-5.0 mmol/L. Potassium
levels are kept in a fairly narrow range because both high and low levels can
affect heart rhythm. A lower-than-usual level (hypokalemia) is often associated
with certain diuretic medications, vomiting, diarrhea, or inadequate intake. A
higher-than-usual level (hyperkalemia) is often associated with reduced kidney
function, certain medications (including some blood pressure medications), or
conditions affecting how the body regulates potassium.

Because potassium imbalances - especially high levels - can affect heart rhythm
and be medically significant, any notably abnormal result is generally treated
as something to discuss with a doctor promptly rather than waiting for a routine
follow-up.
