# HDL Cholesterol

HDL (high-density lipoprotein) is often called "good cholesterol" because it helps
carry excess cholesterol away from artery walls back to the liver for removal.
Higher HDL levels are generally associated with lower cardiovascular risk, which is
the opposite pattern from LDL.

As a general guide, HDL levels above 60 mg/dL are often considered protective,
while levels below 40 mg/dL in men or below 50 mg/dL in women are often associated
with higher cardiovascular risk. Values in between are typically considered
average.

Lifestyle factors such as regular physical activity, not smoking, and certain
dietary patterns are commonly associated with higher HDL, while a sedentary
lifestyle and smoking are commonly associated with lower HDL. As with other lipid
markers, HDL is best interpreted as part of a full lipid panel and overall health
picture rather than alone, and any concerns should be discussed with a doctor.
