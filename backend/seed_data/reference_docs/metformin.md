# Metformin

Metformin is one of the most commonly prescribed medications for type 2 diabetes.
It works mainly by reducing how much glucose the liver releases into the blood
and by improving how the body's cells respond to insulin, which helps lower
blood sugar levels.

It is often taken with food to reduce common digestive side effects such as
nausea, stomach upset, or diarrhea, which tend to improve over the first few
weeks. A rare but serious side effect is lactic acidosis, which is why metformin
is often used cautiously or avoided in people with significant kidney or liver
problems, and why combining it with heavy alcohol use is generally discouraged.

Metformin does not typically cause low blood sugar on its own the way insulin or
some other diabetes medications can, since it doesn't directly increase insulin
production. As with any medication, dosing and suitability depend on individual
health factors like kidney function, and any questions about starting, stopping,
or adjusting it should go through a doctor.
