# Fasting Blood Glucose

Fasting blood glucose measures the amount of sugar in the blood after not eating
for at least 8 hours, and is one of the standard tests used to screen for
prediabetes and diabetes.

Commonly used reference ranges are: below 100 mg/dL as normal, 100-125 mg/dL as
often associated with prediabetes, and 126 mg/dL or above on more than one
occasion as often associated with diabetes. Levels can be temporarily affected by
stress, illness, recent food or caffeine intake, or certain medications, which is
why results are often confirmed with a repeat test or paired with HbA1c.

Persistently elevated fasting glucose is often associated with insulin
resistance or reduced insulin production over time, and is generally interpreted
alongside HbA1c, weight, family history, and other cardiovascular risk factors.
Any abnormal result should be discussed with a doctor rather than acted on
alone.
