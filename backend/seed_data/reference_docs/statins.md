# Statins (e.g., Atorvastatin, Simvastatin, Rosuvastatin)

Statins are a class of medications commonly prescribed to lower LDL ("bad")
cholesterol and reduce the risk of heart attack and stroke, particularly in
people with elevated cholesterol or other cardiovascular risk factors. They work
by blocking an enzyme the liver uses to produce cholesterol.

Common side effects include muscle aches, and in rare cases more significant
muscle breakdown (rhabdomyolysis), which is why unusual muscle pain or weakness
while on a statin is often flagged as something to mention to a doctor. Statins
are also processed by the liver, so liver function is sometimes monitored,
especially when starting treatment or adjusting doses.

Certain other medications and even some foods (notably grapefruit, with some
statins) can interact with statins by affecting how they're broken down in the
body, increasing the risk of side effects - which is part of why it's important
for a doctor or pharmacist to have a full, current medication list. Statins are
typically a long-term therapy, and stopping or changing them should be discussed
with a doctor rather than done independently.
