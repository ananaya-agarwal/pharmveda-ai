# Liver Enzymes: ALT and AST

ALT (alanine aminotransferase) and AST (aspartate aminotransferase) are enzymes
found mostly in liver cells (AST is also found in muscle and heart tissue). When
liver cells are damaged or inflamed, these enzymes leak into the bloodstream, so
elevated levels are often associated with liver stress or injury.

Typical adult reference ranges are often roughly 7-56 U/L for ALT and 10-40 U/L
for AST, though exact ranges vary by lab. Mildly elevated levels are often
associated with fatty liver, alcohol use, certain medications, or recent intense
exercise (particularly for AST). More significantly elevated levels are often
associated with viral hepatitis, more advanced fatty liver disease, or other
liver conditions.

Because ALT and AST reflect liver cell stress rather than how well the liver is
functioning overall, they are usually interpreted alongside other liver tests
(such as bilirubin and albumin) and a person's history and symptoms. Any elevated
result should be discussed with a doctor rather than interpreted in isolation.
