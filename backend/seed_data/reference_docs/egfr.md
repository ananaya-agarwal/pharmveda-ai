# eGFR (Estimated Glomerular Filtration Rate)

eGFR is a calculated estimate of how much blood the kidneys filter per minute,
derived from a blood creatinine level combined with age, sex, and sometimes race
or body size. It is one of the most commonly used indicators of overall kidney
function.

An eGFR of 90 or above is often considered a sign of normal or high kidney function,
though this can vary with age since eGFR naturally tends to decline somewhat over
time. Values consistently between 60 and 89 may simply reflect mild, age-related
decline, while values below 60 sustained over three months or more are often
associated with chronic kidney disease staging. Values below 15 are often
associated with significantly reduced kidney function requiring closer medical
attention.

Because eGFR is an estimate rather than a direct measurement, and because it can
fluctuate with hydration, recent illness, or muscle mass, trends over multiple
tests are generally more informative than a single result. Any sustained decline
should be discussed with a doctor.
