# Beta Blockers (e.g., Metoprolol, Atenolol, Propranolol)

Beta blockers are commonly prescribed for high blood pressure, certain heart
rhythm problems, after a heart attack, and for some other conditions like
migraine prevention or anxiety-related tremor. They work by blocking the effects
of adrenaline-related hormones on the heart, which slows heart rate and reduces
how hard the heart has to work.

Because they slow heart rate, beta blockers are generally avoided or used
carefully in combination with other medications that also slow the heart, such
as certain calcium channel blockers, since combining them can cause the heart
rate to drop too low. They can also mask some of the usual warning signs of low
blood sugar (like a racing heart) in people with diabetes, which is useful
context for anyone managing both conditions.

Stopping a beta blocker abruptly can be risky and is generally not recommended,
since it can cause a rebound increase in heart rate or blood pressure - dose
changes should always be guided by a doctor rather than done independently.
