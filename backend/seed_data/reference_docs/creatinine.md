# Creatinine

Creatinine is a waste product created by normal muscle activity and filtered out of
the blood by the kidneys. Because the kidneys are responsible for clearing it, blood
creatinine levels are commonly used as a rough indicator of how well the kidneys are
filtering waste.

Typical reference ranges vary by lab, sex, age, and muscle mass, but adult values
roughly between 0.6 and 1.3 mg/dL are often considered within the usual range. A
higher-than-usual creatinine level is often associated with reduced kidney filtering
ability, dehydration, a high-protein diet, intense exercise, or certain medications.
A lower-than-usual level is often associated with lower muscle mass and is less often
clinically significant on its own.

Creatinine is frequently interpreted together with eGFR (estimated glomerular
filtration rate), which adjusts for age and sex to give a fuller picture of kidney
function. A single elevated creatinine reading is not enough to draw conclusions from
- trends over time and other kidney-related tests matter, and any concerning result
should be discussed with a doctor.
