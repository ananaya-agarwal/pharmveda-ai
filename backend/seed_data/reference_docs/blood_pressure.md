# Blood Pressure Readings

Blood pressure is reported as two numbers: systolic (the pressure when the heart
beats) over diastolic (the pressure when the heart rests between beats), measured
in mmHg.

Commonly used general categories are: below 120/80 mmHg as normal, 120-129
systolic with under 80 diastolic as elevated, 130-139/80-89 mmHg as often
associated with stage 1 high blood pressure, and 140/90 mmHg or above as often
associated with stage 2 high blood pressure. A single high reading isn't
unusual - stress, recent caffeine, or measurement technique can all cause
temporary spikes - so trends across multiple readings, ideally at rest and at
different times, matter more than one number.

Sustained high blood pressure over time is often associated with increased risk
of heart disease, stroke, and kidney problems, which is why it's often called a
"silent" risk factor - it frequently causes no symptoms until it has been
elevated for a long time. Any consistently elevated readings should be discussed
with a doctor.
